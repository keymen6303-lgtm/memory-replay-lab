# Results and interpretation

These are original local stage 7–9 result snapshots, not new independent replications performed by external researchers. The repository preserves the original model and protocol files. Each stage uses different confirmation seeds, so do not interpret differences between stage means as paired interventions.

## Stage 7: energy-guided selection

The closed-form paper identities and replay-gain ordering were reproduced in the specified synthetic geometry. In the main confirmation condition, high-energy rather than random selection improved partial-cue identity recovery by 0.30 percentage points (97.5% paired bootstrap interval: 0.125 to 0.50 points). Similar-input false alarms decreased by 0.90 points (interval: −1.30 to −0.55 points). The prescribed hypothesis predicted improved recovery **and increased** false alarms; it was not confirmed. Recovery was only 4.525% with high-energy selection. This is a replication plus a narrow boundary experiment, not a new energy-based memory algorithm.

## Stage 8: recognition readouts on fixed memory

For the high-energy arm, weighted energy yielded AUC 0.77568, false alarms 33.95%, old-target hits 86.70%, and partial identity recovery 4.75%. Maximum similarity yielded AUC 0.78878, false alarms 32.70% and hits 86.10%. The joint criterion was not established because the hit noninferiority requirement was not established. Equal mass changed little; reconstruction performed worse. All four readouts misrecognized all 640 similar queries around the eight replay-buffer parents, motivating the next source control.

## Stage 9: source controls

Primary readout: weighted energy. Main angle: 0.24 radians. High-energy selection:

| Replay source | AUC | Similar false alarms | Old hits | Partial identity recovery |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Exact old vectors | 0.77510 | 34.50% | 90.60% | 4.575% |
| Common-rotation vectors | 0.76968 | 35.85% | 89.80% | 4.575% |
| Angle-matched independent directions | 0.77438 | 34.50% | 91.40% | 4.525% |

Independent minus exact source: AUC −0.00072 (99.642857% interval −0.00162 to 0.00014); false-alarm difference approximately 0 (−0.45 to 0.50 percentage points). The prescribed five-endpoint joint improvement criterion failed. Removing exact old copies did not remove weighted-energy false recognition in these conditions. This revises the simple exact-copy explanation; it does not prove source precision is irrelevant in all settings.

High-energy versus random selection showed small favorable differences within both exact and independent source conditions. The interaction intervals crossed zero: this does not establish equivalence or general robustness. With all three sources, weighted energy misrecognized all 640 buffered-parent similar inputs.

Exploratory maximum-similarity comparison: exact sources gave 33.40% false alarms and 90.60% hits; independent sources gave 3.40% false alarms and 88.50% hits. The false-alarm reduction came with a 2.10-point hit decrease (unadjusted exploratory 95% interval −3.40 to −0.90 points). The common-rotation source has identical maximum-similarity scores to no replay because it duplicates existing support points; that equality is an algebraic identity, not a new empirical discovery.

## Limits

- Synthetic, fixed-encoder, closed-form explicit-vector memories; no evidence about human neurons, STDP or trained deep networks.
- Replay arms share selection/event/storage budgets; no-replay has a lower budget.
- Geometry changes affect support duplication or inter-source correlation as well as old-source similarity.
- Thresholds come from calibration old-target scores; they are group/readout-specific and fixed before confirmation. No guarantee of deployment calibration under other distributions.
- Seed-level paired bootstrap intervals are approximate and prespecified family adjustments apply only to primary comparisons. Exploratory findings require a fresh protocol and confirmation study.
- Low identity recovery and weaker discrimination than the lower-budget no-replay reference preclude presenting the toolkit as an effective general memory system.
- The literature search is finite and some sources were only partially readable. No global-first claim is made.

Machine-readable summaries, seed-level tables, primary intervals and frozen thresholds are in `reference_results/`. Raw query/event tables and NPZ caches are regenerated by the full entry points rather than shipped in the first public release.
